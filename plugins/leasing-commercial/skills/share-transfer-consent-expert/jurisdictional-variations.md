# Share Transfer Consent — Jurisdictional Variations & Precedent Adaptation

The base precedent contemplated in this skill is for **Ontario commercial leases**. Adapt as follows for other jurisdictions and deal structures.

## Jurisdictional Variations

### Quebec
- Civil Code references instead of common law
- Different privacy legislation (Bill 64 — Law 25)
- Two-witness requirement for individual signatories
- French language version may be required (Charter of the French Language)

### Other Common Law Provinces (BC, Alberta, etc.)
- Provincial corporate statutes differ (BCBCA, ABCA, etc. vs. OBCA/CBCA)
- Privacy legislation varies (PIPA in BC/AB vs. PIPEDA federally)
- Court jurisdiction clauses adjusted to local superior court
- Governing law clause changed
- Familial/affiliate exemption language may differ in statute

### United States
- State-specific corporate law (Delaware DGCL most common for entity formation)
- Federal and state privacy laws (CCPA in California, etc.)
- Different signature block conventions (no "I have authority to bind" formula)
- Notarization may be required for recordable instruments
- Consideration clause may use $10.00 instead of $2.00

## Corporate Law Considerations (All Jurisdictions)
- **Constating Documents**: Ensure share transfer permitted by articles/bylaws
- **Director Approval**: Board resolution authorizing transfer
- **Securities Law**: Compliance with securities regulations if applicable
- **Shareholder Agreement**: Existing shareholders may have rights of first refusal
- **Business Corporation Act**: Statutory requirements for share transfers

## Sector-Specific Adaptation

- **Retail**: Address tenant mix and exclusive use provisions
- **Industrial**: Emphasize hazmat/environmental compliance continuity
- **Office**: Professional image and building standard provisions
- **Ground Lease**: More stringent due diligence and landlord consent provisions

## Deal Structure Adaptation

- **Partial Transfer**: Adjust for less than 100% share transfer; recital must specify percentage
- **Multiple Shareholders**: More complex shareholding chart; may need shareholders' agreement copy
- **Indirect Transfer**: Change in parent company or upstream shareholder — verify "change of control" definition in lease captures upstream events
- **Merger/Acquisition**: Different structure than simple share purchase — consider whether tenant entity survives or is rolled into acquirer

## Permitted Transfer Carve-Outs (Often Exempt from Consent)
- Family transfers (spouse, children)
- Affiliate transfers (parent/subsidiary, common control)
- Transfers to public company shareholders (if below control threshold)
- Estate planning transfers (trusts, holdcos)

## Reasonableness Standard by Jurisdiction
- Some leases: landlord consent "not to be unreasonably withheld"
- Other leases: landlord has "sole and absolute discretion"
- Reasonableness may be implied by law even if not stated — Ontario courts generally imply a reasonableness standard absent clear "sole discretion" language; US states vary widely

## Timing & Process

**Typical Timeline** (build buffer into share purchase closing date):
1. Tenant notice to landlord: Minimum 15-30 days before closing
2. Landlord due diligence: 10-15 business days
3. Consent negotiation: 5-10 business days
4. Agreement execution: 3-5 business days
5. **Total**: 30-45 days

**Process Steps**:
1. Tenant provides written notice with required information
2. Landlord reviews and conducts due diligence
3. Landlord responds (consent, conditional consent, or refusal)
4. Parties negotiate terms of consent agreement
5. Consent agreement executed by all three parties
6. Share transfer closes
7. Tenant notifies landlord of completion
